The risk

Why first surface mirrors can't be cleaned like glass

A first surface mirror is a precision optic, not a window. The wrong touch changes what your pilots see.

The coating is on top

The reflective layer sits exposed on the surface, not protected behind glass. It scratches far more easily than an ordinary mirror.

Wrong chemicals cloud the image

Ammonia, solvents, abrasives and all-purpose cleaners can haze or strip the coating and degrade the image pilots train on.

Dust and haze erode fidelity

Even light buildup lowers brightness, contrast and clarity. Over time it quietly reduces training value.

The Clear Sky standard: simulator-safe, non-abrasive methods, with the right tools, sequence and inspection at every step.

FAQ

Questions, answered clearly

How often should first surface mirrors be cleaned?

Most active training centers plan routine cleaning every 3 to 6 months. High-use devices, dusty environments, nearby construction or visible haze call for a shorter interval. Your quote can include a maintenance rhythm matched to your simulator use.

Do you need the simulator powered down?

No. The simulator does not need to be powered down. It just can't be in flight or in use for training during the visit. Your normal access procedure for the visual-system bay is all that's required.

How long does a visit take?

An in-person evaluation takes about 30 minutes per device. Cleaning time depends on the simulator's size and model, and on the number and size of its mirrors, so it's quoted per device. Either way, work is scheduled around your training blocks.

Do you clean other optics?

Yes. Beyond first surface mirrors, Clear Sky cleans beam splitter and combiner glass, back and front projection screens and chin window fold mirrors, and handles moth remediation inside visual-system enclosures.
